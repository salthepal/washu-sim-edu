## Name the pattern before reacting

A learner may be quiet, defensive, dismissive, anxious, unsafe, overconfident, or
disengaged. Those behaviors feel different in the room, but they all require the
facilitator to slow down and identify what is actually observable.

Avoid jumping from behavior to motive. "The learner did not verbalize a plan" is
more useful than "the learner did not care." Observable language keeps the
debrief fair and gives the learner something concrete to respond to.

## Use curiosity before correction

Many difficult moments are frame problems. The learner may have misunderstood the
case, felt exposed, focused on a different priority, or believed they were doing
what the team needed. Start by exploring the learner's frame before giving a
correction.

Useful facilitator moves include:

1. Describe the observed behavior.
2. State the concern or performance gap.
3. Ask what the learner was seeing, thinking, or prioritizing.
4. Connect the discussion back to the case objective.

## Escalate when safety requires it

Psychological safety does not mean avoiding hard feedback. If a learner's actions
are unsafe, disrespectful, or repeatedly disruptive, the facilitator should name
the behavior clearly and set expectations for the rest of the session.

For recurrent concerns, document the pattern and involve the appropriate program
leadership. Simulation can reveal important performance gaps, but remediation
should not depend on a single facilitator improvising alone.
