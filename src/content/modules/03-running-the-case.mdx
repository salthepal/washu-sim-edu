## Matching modality to objective

Choose the modality that lets learners practice the target behavior. A
high-fidelity manikin may be useful when changing physiology matters. A task
trainer may be enough for a procedure. A standardized patient or confederate may
be the right choice when communication, uncertainty, or team dynamics are the
center of the case.

Fidelity is a tool, not the goal. The best modality is the one that makes the
objective observable.

## Briefing SPs, confederates, and embedded participants

Embedded participants need a clear role, emotional tone, boundaries, and cueing
plan. They should know what information to offer freely, what to reveal only if
asked, when to escalate, and what behaviors would derail the learning
environment.

Briefing is especially important when a participant is meant to create pressure.
The pressure should serve the objective without overwhelming the fiction
contract or psychological safety.

## Dry runs and case logistics

A dry run checks whether the written case works in the actual room. Use it to
test equipment, slides, monitors, microphones, task trainers, medications,
props, timing, and handoffs between facilitators.

Dry runs also reveal whether the case is too crowded. If the facilitator needs
too many simultaneous cues, changes, or technology moves, simplify the case
before learners arrive.

## Managing technology and real-time problems

Cases rarely run exactly as written. Learners may get stuck, chase a trivial
detail, avoid touching the patient, or move faster than expected. Technology may
fail. A facilitator should know which elements are essential to preserve and
which can be adapted in the moment.

When something breaks, protect the objective first. Pause, redirect, simplify,
or narrate a missing finding if that keeps the case educationally intact.
