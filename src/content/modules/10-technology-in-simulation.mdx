## Let the objective choose the technology

Simulation technology should make learner performance observable. It should not
be the center of the case unless the objective is actually about using that
technology.

Start with the learner behavior you need to see. A communication objective may
need a standardized participant more than a high-fidelity manikin. A procedural
objective may need a task trainer and a clear scoring plan. A team leadership
objective may need a realistic monitor, noise, task load, and confederate roles.

## Choose fidelity deliberately

Fidelity is not one thing. A case may need physiologic fidelity, environmental
fidelity, emotional fidelity, equipment fidelity, or team fidelity. More fidelity
is not automatically better. Extra realism can add cognitive load, distract from
the objective, or create technical failure points.

For each technology choice, ask:

1. What learner action does this make possible or visible?
2. What could go wrong during setup or runtime?
3. What can be simplified without weakening the objective?

## Build a backup plan

Technology-dependent cases should include a facilitator backup plan. Decide what
the facilitator will say or do if the monitor freezes, a manikin function fails,
an audiovisual feed drops, or learners cannot interact with the equipment as
expected.

If the backup plan preserves the same decision point and debrief hook, the case
can still teach even when the technology is imperfect.
